I gave a contributed talk at the 28th International Conference on High Resolution Molecular Spectroscopy (HRMS 2026) in Prague, 31 August to 4 September 2026. The talk was in the (Exo)planetary Atmospheres mini-symposium on the first morning. My co-authors are Jonathan Tennyson and Sergei Yurchenko (UCL, ExoMol).

Minor isotopologues often have few measured energy levels, so their line lists depend mostly on calculation. Isotopologue extrapolation takes the known error in the calculated levels of the main isotopologue and moves that error onto the minor ones. We train a neural network to correct what this method leaves behind. For CO2, it cut the mean absolute error by 82% and improved 87% of levels. Transfer learning from CO2 to CO gave a similar 82% cut. The full results are in our [J. Mol. Spectrosc. paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jms.2026.112084).

I also showed two pieces of work in progress. The first is a graph neural network version of isotopologue extrapolation. The second is MARVEL-GNN, which checks the experimental data before it goes into a line list. We trained one network on the MARVEL networks of three CO isotopologues and tested it on two isotopologues it had not seen. Its uncertainty estimates match the real errors much better than the current MARVEL method. It can also flag bad lines, suggest corrected quantum-number labels, and suggest where detached parts of a network should reconnect.

These models are only as good as the experimental levels we train them on. If you have unpublished isotopologue data, please [get in touch](/).
